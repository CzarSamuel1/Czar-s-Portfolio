## Context

DeySure is a Nigerian-first record-keeping app for agreements, invoices,
reminders, and documents — built for informal arrangements (a WhatsApp
promise, a handshake) as much as formal contracts. It's a standalone
product I designed and built myself, not an internal tool.

## The problem

Most agreements people actually make don't come with paperwork — a text,
a voice note, a verbal promise. The problem isn't the agreement itself,
it's remembering it later: what was promised, to whom, for how much, and
by when.

## Key decisions

### Money tracking is secondary, on purpose

The invoice section reads: *"Invoices are secondary — generate one only
if you actually need to bill this agreement."* Most agreements in
DeySure never need an invoice at all — the product resists becoming
accounting software by default.

### A plain-language record, not a form

An agreement's headline reads as a sentence — "Mickkystore owes you
₦400,000.00, due 29 Aug 2026" — rather than a generic record label. The
product mirrors how people actually describe what they're owed.

### Meeting people where the agreement happened

A functional "Chat on WhatsApp" action sits directly on the agreement
detail view, alongside Follow up, Mark paid, and Mark completed —
reflecting that most of these agreements started as a WhatsApp message
in the first place.
